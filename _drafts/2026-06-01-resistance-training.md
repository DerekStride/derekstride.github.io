# Resistance Training

## Key Concepts

The 2 key concepts that you have to understand to get effective results in resistance training is Progressive Overload and Deloading.

Progressive Overload can be summed up as increasing the intensity of a workout every session. You have 2 main ways to increase intensity, the first is to increase the weight and the second is to increase the number of reps. You only build muscle and strength when you're training is challenging, by continously increasing the intensity you'll be signalling your body that it needs to adapt.

Deloading is the opposite factor that keeps progressive overload in check. After increasing the intesity week over week, you'll reach a point where the workout will be too challenging to complete. If you don't meet your target rep count that is a sign it's time to deload by decreasing the weight or number of reps. Deloading is a key component to an effective plan & let's your body recover so you can continue pushing yourself in the future.

## Goals & Benefits

There are plenty of benefits to resistance training and it's recommended that adults do 2 resistance training sessions a week.

Among with all the health benefits there are 2 common goals to resistance training, building strength and building muscle. Each have their own training style.

For building strength you want to lift heavy weights for sets with low rep counts. Traditionally, this is 5 or fewer reps.

For building muscle you want to train until failure, that is, you want to do as many reps as possible before you physically can't do more. It's important that they are quality reps, once your form breaks down that's when you should stop. It's also a good to have enough weight where you physically can't do more than 30 reps.

## Measuring Success

As with any measurement, there are [leading and lagging measures](/books/deep-work). The lagging measures are obvious here, it takes a long time to build muscle mass & strength. If you look more muscular or can lift more weight than in the past these are good indicators that your training has been working.

However, tracking leading measures will often lead to improvements in the lagging measures. The weight, reps, & duration of your training are all good leading measurements. Tracking your progress week to week and sticking to the Progressive Overload principles will help motivate you and give you a target to aim for each workout.
